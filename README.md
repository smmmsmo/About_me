# About Mo

> I do not want technology to feel like magic. I want to understand what is happening underneath, build things myself, and keep learning for as long as I live.

My name is **SM Mostofa Morshed Sayeem**, but call me **Mo**—naturally, as a friend would.

I am a BSc Computer Science student, practical builder, Linux user, open-education advocate, and lifelong learner. My relationship with technology began by helping people in and around my rural village solve everyday problems. I now explore programming, systems, backend development, databases, automation, cybersecurity, open source, research, and the relationship between technology and a meaningful life.

This repository is the evolving content foundation for a future personal website. It distinguishes what I know, what I have practised, what I am actively learning, what I have built, and what I only hope to explore later.

## Education and Aspiration

I love MIT's systems, course design, clarity, innovation, and commitment to accessible knowledge through **MIT OpenCourseWare**. I also learn through **Open Source Society University (OSSU)**. Studying at MIT is a serious future aspiration, while contributing to open education is part of the person I hope to become.

Before graduating, I want to build projects I understand deeply, strengthen mathematics, aim to publish two or three papers, and become ready for graduate study abroad.

## Life Beyond the Screen

I want technology to protect time rather than consume it. My long-term hope is stable engineering work, a loving family, personal systems that monitor themselves, and enough freedom to be a present husband and father.

I also hope eventually to build a responsible farm and food business that cares for soil, animals, customers, workers, family, and the environment.

## Central Principle

I care more about usefulness than scale. A free tool that helps me and four or five other people can be a successful project.

I want to understand, build, debug, deploy, share, teach, care, and keep climbing.

> The climb makes the view sweeter.
