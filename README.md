# About Mo

> I do not want technology to feel like magic. I want to understand what is happening underneath, build things myself, and keep learning for as long as I live.

My name is **SM Mostofa Morshed Sayeem**, but friends—and AI assistants working with me—should simply call me **Mo**.

I am a BSc Computer Science student with roughly two years remaining in my degree. I am still building practical ability, and I prefer an honest description of where I stand over an exaggerated list of skills.

## Why Computer Science?

My earliest fascination came from a childhood question: how can people speak from different places and have their voices transmitted accurately?

That curiosity grew into a desire to understand and work with the systems around me instead of treating them as unexplained tools. I see computer science as a foundational field for doing that. It connects communication, automation, artificial intelligence, networking, software, and many other things I want to explore.

I am a curious person with many interests. Automation was once my main direction, although I am still discovering which field should receive most of my attention. My current interests include:

- artificial intelligence and machine learning;
- computer vision and image processing;
- networking;
- automation; and
- software development.

## What I Want to Achieve Before Graduation

Over the next two years, I want to:

1. Build meaningful projects that I genuinely understand.
2. Learn to design and construct things myself instead of only following finished solutions.
3. Aim to publish two or three research papers to develop real research skills.
4. Strengthen my mathematical foundations for graduate study, AI, and research.
5. Continue competitive programming occasionally for enjoyment.
6. Become properly prepared and confident for a master's degree abroad.

I am considering the United States and other Western destinations, including Europe, for graduate study. I do not want my present institution or location included in this profile.

## How I Learn Best

I learn best when a subject is handled in a clear sequence:

1. Establish the necessary prerequisites.
2. Explain one part thoroughly.
3. Use relatable examples and visuals whenever they improve understanding.
4. Show how the idea works in practice.
5. Move to the next part only after the current part has been explained properly.

I do **not** want constant quizzes or unnecessary questions during a lesson. I prefer learning from fundamentals, examples, careful explanations, and practical work.

For programming and projects, explain:

- **what** the code or function does;
- **why** it is needed;
- **how** it works; and
- **why it belongs in that particular part of the project**.

A complete, heavily documented reference project can be valuable to me. I want to study how it works and then build it myself so that the final understanding becomes mine.

## How to Work With Me

- Call me **Mo**, naturally and like a friend.
- Teach one part at a time and keep the current scope clear.
- Give me prerequisites instead of assuming that I already know them.
- Prefer useful visuals and relatable examples when possible.
- Be direct and critical when I am mistaken.
- Clearly tell me what is wrong, why it is wrong, and how to correct it.
- Do not protect me from useful criticism or agree with me merely to be pleasant.
- Avoid unnecessary discussion and unexplained complexity.
- Help me understand and build; do not quietly replace my learning with completed work.
- Never confuse my curiosity about a field with expertise in it.

## Strengths and Growth Areas

I see myself as a kind and curious person. I enjoy learning, I am willing to begin as a beginner, and I want to understand things deeply.

My current challenges are:

- having too many interests at once;
- weak mathematical foundations;
- inconsistency; and
- lack of confidence.

These are not excuses or permanent labels. They are areas I want to work on honestly.

Ultimately, I am trying to become someone I can look back on with pride.

## Beyond Computer Science

I used to read many books, although I currently read very little. Music is now one of my main personal interests. I occasionally watch anime in English dub, and I am learning Japanese—perhaps one day I will need neither a dub nor subtitles.

My ambitions are intentionally broader than a single career. If I establish a stable income, I would like to build a food- or crop-related business. Much later in life, after advanced academic study, I may also explore becoming a medical doctor in a non-surgical area.

I plan to study throughout my lifetime. Computer science is my present foundation, not necessarily the boundary of everything I may pursue.
