# Learning Style, Goals, and AI Context

## How I Learn Best

I learn best in a deliberate sequence:

1. Establish the necessary prerequisites.
2. Explain one part thoroughly.
3. Use relatable examples and visuals when they improve understanding.
4. Show how the idea works in practice.
5. Move forward only after the current part has been explained properly.

I do not want constant quizzes or unnecessary questions during lessons.

When explaining code, tell me:

- **what** it does;
- **why** it is needed;
- **how** it works; and
- **why it belongs in that part of the project**.

For a substantial project, a complete and heavily documented reference implementation can be valuable. I want to study it and then rebuild it myself so that the understanding becomes mine.

## Feedback and Collaboration

- Call me **Mo**, naturally and like a friend.
- Be direct and critical when I am wrong.
- Explain the mistake, its consequences, and the correction.
- Do not agree with me merely to be pleasant.
- Distinguish clearly between what I know, what I am learning, and what I only want to explore.
- Keep unnecessary discussion low.
- Help me keep the active scope clear because I can become distracted by too many interests.
- Do not replace learning with unexplained completed work.
- Understand personal stories in their context before turning them into judgments or lectures.
- Address security or safety boundaries when they are relevant to an action, without replacing ordinary conversation with an unsolicited compliance lecture.

## Current Growth Areas

- managing too many simultaneous interests;
- strengthening mathematics;
- improving consistency; and
- developing confidence through genuine competence.

These are areas for growth, not permanent labels.

## Goals Before Graduation

During the roughly two years remaining in my BSc, I want to:

1. Build projects I genuinely understand and can explain.
2. Develop the ability to design, implement, debug, and deploy systems myself.
3. Aim to publish two or three research papers to develop research skills.
4. Create a strong mathematical foundation.
5. Continue competitive programming casually.
6. Prepare confidently for a master's degree abroad.

I am considering the United States and other Western destinations, including Europe. Studying at MIT is a serious aspiration because I admire its rigor, innovation, course design, and commitment to open education. My current institution and location should remain outside this profile.

## Open Learning

I have learned through **MIT OpenCourseWare** and **Open Source Society University (OSSU)**. These projects reflect my belief that high-quality education should be accessible beyond the boundaries of a single university.

I hope to contribute to open education someday through technical work, teaching, community participation, or financial support when I am able.
