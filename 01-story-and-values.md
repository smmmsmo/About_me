# Story and Values

> The climb makes the view sweeter.

## Who I Am

My name is **SM Mostofa Morshed Sayeem**, but friends and AI assistants should call me **Mo**.

I am a BSc Computer Science student with roughly two years remaining. I am curious, kind, and interested in learning throughout my life. I want an honest record of my abilities and ambitions: what I hope to learn should never be presented as something I have already mastered.

## Where My Relationship With Technology Began

I grew up in a rural village where many people had limited experience with technology. People from my village and surrounding areas came to me for help with everyday problems: saving a phone number, changing a phone from silent to normal mode, recovering access to their own accounts, and fixing other small technological issues.

These tasks were technically simple, but they mattered to the people who needed them. I did not charge for the help. I enjoyed understanding the problem, solving it, and seeing someone leave less confused than when they arrived.

This is an important reason I chose computer science. I did not become interested in technology only because I liked machines. Understanding technology made me useful to people.

Another childhood question also stayed with me: how can people speak from different places and have their voices transmitted accurately? That question developed into a desire to understand what happens beneath the surface of the systems around me.

## What I Value

### Useful work

I do not need to build something used by millions of people. If I create a tool that genuinely helps me and four or five other people, that is meaningful to me.

### Open source

I have benefited from Linux and other open-source work. I want to contribute back instead of remaining only a consumer. Useful software does not always need to be created for profit.

### Accessible education

I believe education should be free and accessible. I hope to teach children someday and help them approach knowledge without unnecessary fear.

Two open-education systems have influenced me directly:

- **MIT OpenCourseWare (MIT OCW)** gives independent learners access to carefully designed MIT course materials.
- **Open Source Society University (OSSU)** organizes high-quality, freely accessible resources into a structured computer science curriculum.

I have learned from MIT OCW and OSSU. I hope to contribute to the open-education ecosystem someday through my time, technical work, teaching, community participation, or financial support when I am able.

### Why MIT Matters to Me

I love MIT—not only as a famous university, but as a system and an educational philosophy. I admire its course design, clarity, innovation, technical culture, and willingness to share knowledge openly through MIT OCW.

Studying at MIT in the future is a serious aspiration. It is not presented as a guaranteed destination. The aspiration represents the standard of rigor, openness, and innovation toward which I want to work.

### Kindness and care

My instinct to help is not limited to technology. I feed stray dogs by my own choice. Helping a person or animal in need makes me feel genuinely good.

The same value shapes my [life and family vision](07-life-and-family-vision.md) and my hope to build a [responsible farm and food business](08-farm-business-and-stewardship.md). I want competence and stability to increase my ability to care for people, animals, and the environment—not separate me from them.

## The Person I Want to Become

I am trying to become someone I can look back on with pride. I do not expect that growth to happen quickly. I want to build ability gradually, remain curious, take responsibility seriously, and keep studying throughout my life.
