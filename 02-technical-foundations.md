## Device and Operating-System Environment

I learn across several devices:

- **MacBook Air M4** — my primary development machine;
- **Arch Linux laptop** — Arch is my favourite Linux distribution;
- **Raspberry Pi 4B** — runs my Debian server, Sparkle;
- **OnePlus 8T** — my current phone and part of my everyday device ecosystem.

I deliberately chose Linux because I had heard it was difficult. I wanted to understand it instead of remaining afraid of it.

## Linux and Server Administration

I am confident as a Linux user and now have early hands-on server-administration experience. I still do not claim mastery.

On my Raspberry Pi server **Sparkle**, I have practised:

- installing and maintaining Debian GNU/Linux;
- SSH login and public-key authentication;
- hostname and local-network access;
- users, groups, sudo access, permissions, and ACLs;
- shared workspaces;
- packages and services;
- systemd and logs;
- Fail2Ban and unattended security updates;
- network and resource monitoring;
- troubleshooting configuration and service problems; and
- documenting the system for future use and for other learners.

Sensitive server addresses, account details, and internal configuration are intentionally excluded from this public profile.

## SSH and Multiple Devices

I originally approached SSH because I wanted to manage multiple devices from one place. I have now used it repeatedly, configured key-based access, and relied on it while administering Sparkle.

The accurate description is no longer “I have only tried SSH a few times.” I have practical familiarity and growing confidence, while advanced remote administration and security remain active learning areas.

## Networking

My interest in networking connects directly to my earliest curiosity about communication across distance. I want to understand how devices discover, reach, authenticate, and communicate with one another—not merely memorize terminology.

Sparkle gives me a practical environment for local networking, SSH, service exposure, port checks, monitoring, and troubleshooting.

## Databases and Platforms

I am learning PostgreSQL and database fundamentals. My goal is to understand how applications store, organize, query, protect, and maintain real information.

I also have practical project exposure to **Supabase** and **Vercel**. Using managed platforms is not the same as mastering databases, cloud infrastructure, or DevOps, so I keep those claims separate.

## Guiding Principle

I do not want technology to feel like magic. I want to understand what is happening underneath, use it deliberately, and eventually manage or build it without depending blindly on someone else.
