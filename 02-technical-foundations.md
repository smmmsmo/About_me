## Linux

I chose to use Linux deliberately because I had heard that it was difficult. I wanted to understand what it was and how it worked instead of remaining afraid of it.

I am confident using Linux, but confidence as a user is not the same as mastery of system administration. I am continuing to learn how services, permissions, processes, networking, logs, packages, and deployments work underneath the interface.

## SSH and Multiple Devices

I own and use multiple devices, and I want to manage them from a single place. This motivated my interest in SSH, remote administration, and networking.

I have used SSH several times, but I am not yet fully confident that I can configure and manage everything independently without guidance or administrative services. SSH therefore belongs in my **active learning** category, not my mastered-skills category.

## Networking

My interest in networking connects directly to my earliest curiosity about communication across distance. I want to understand how devices discover, reach, authenticate, and communicate with one another—not merely memorize networking terminology.

## Databases

I am learning PostgreSQL and database fundamentals. My goal is to understand how applications store, organize, query, protect, and maintain real information.

## Guiding Principle

I do not want technology to feel like magic. I want to understand what is happening underneath, use it deliberately, and eventually manage or build it without depending blindly on someone else.
